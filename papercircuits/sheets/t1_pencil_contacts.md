## You need

- 5 mm copper tape and clear tape
- A 6B and an HB pencil, sharpened, and an eraser
- A few centimetres of bare wire, or cut-off resistor legs, for K4
- A multimeter with 200 Ω and 200 kΩ ranges
- Your T0 results: O1 should behave like T0's J2

## Before you start

1. Print sheet 1 at 100 % on card. Write the paper on it: brand, weight and finish.
2. Zero the meter on its 200 Ω range and write the reading on sheet 1.
3. Keep fingers off the glue except where a step says to treat it: skin oil changes results.

## 1 · Tape overlaps

1. Lay the left-hand piece of each lane from A to just past J.
2. Cut the right-hand piece and peel its backing. Treat the glue on its first 10 mm: **scratched**
   means a criss-cross scored with a fingernail; **pencil** means rubbing the tip back and forth until
   the glue looks grey.
3. Lay it over the left-hand piece by 10 mm, from J to B, and burnish the overlap hard with a
   fingernail.
4. **O6, sticky to sticky:** fold the left-hand piece's last 10 mm back over itself so its glue faces
   up. Rub 6B into that glue and into the right-hand piece's glue, then lay the right-hand piece on
   top.
5. Brush loose graphite off the paper. A smudge beside a lane is a leak.
6. Measure A to B on the 200 Ω range. **Flexed** and **Day 7** work as on T0.

## 2 · Tape on pencil

1. Shade each track with the 6B: at least 10 passes, until it shines.
2. Join copper to both ends of each track. **K1:** lay the tape straight on. **K2:** scratch and rub
   6B into the glue's end first. **K3:** fold the tape's last 10 mm back on itself, sticky to sticky,
   so copper faces down; hold it on the pencil with clear tape. **K4:** stop the tape short, and
   bridge to the pencil with bare wire under clear tape. **K5:** your idea.
3. Switch to the 200 kΩ range. Measure A to B, then the bare pencil between the two small circles.
4. **Day 7:** measure A to B again.

## Pass or fail

- **Overlaps:** pass if within 1 Ω of O0 and steady. If O3 to O6 pass where O1 fails,
  pencil-treated overlaps could replace solder.
- **On pencil:** a good join makes A to B only a little more than the pencil-only reading. Several
  times more, OL, or a wandering reading means a poor contact.

## Trying different papers

Paper changes how pencil takes, how well tape grips and how flaps spring back. Build sheet 1 on each
paper you're considering and compare:

- Smooth card, 160–200 gsm: the standard's choice
- Heavier card, 250–300 gsm, if your printer takes it
- Cartridge or drawing paper, 120–160 gsm: more tooth for pencil
- Coated (silk or gloss) card: tape grips well, pencil may not
- Plain 80 gsm paper, as a baseline

The pencil-only reading compares how each paper takes graphite: lower is better. Overlays are a
separate question: try printing on vellum or tracing paper too, as inkjet may smudge.
