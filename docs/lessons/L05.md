# L05 · Two ways to add a resistor — teacher notes

Page: [out/L05-series-parallel.pdf](../../out/L05-series-parallel.pdf). Status: draft. Needs [P1](P1.md) in the binder.

## The idea

All five resistors are the same 470 Ω. They are grouped three ways, each feeding an LED:

- **One:** R1 alone, the reference.
- **Two in a row (series):** R2 then R3. The current has to get through both, so the resistances add up, to about 940 Ω.
- **Two side by side (parallel):** R4 and R5 bridge the same gap. The current has two paths, so the resistance halves, to about 235 Ω.

The LEDs rank side by side (brightest), then one, then in a row (dimmest). Most learners expect "two resistors" always to mean dimmer, so the parallel branch is the surprise.

On the page, R4 and R5 land on the same rail and the same node strip, both passing by. That's how a parallel pair works without branching tape.

## Expected readings

**Resistance:** BT1 off, from TP1 to each point. Resistor tolerance is usually ±5 %.

| From TP1 to | Reading |
|---|---|
| TP2, one | about 470 Ω |
| TP3, two in a row | about 940 Ω |
| TP4, two side by side | about 235 Ω |

The other branches don't upset these readings: the meter's test voltage can't push current backwards through their LEDs.

**Brightness and current** with P1 at 4.5 V:

| Branch | Current | Brightness |
|---|---|---|
| Two side by side | about 11 mA | brightest |
| One | about 5 mA | medium |
| Two in a row | about 2.7 mA | dimmest |

## Common faults

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| TP4 reads about 470 Ω | One of R4 or R5 isn't touching the node strip |
| TP3 reads about 470 Ω | One of R2 or R3 is bridged, or a lead crosses the middle lane's gap |
| Readings drift or look wrong | BT1 is still on. Measure resistance with the power off. |
| LED1 and LED3 look the same | Hard to judge by eye at 5 and 11 mA; cover one at a time |
