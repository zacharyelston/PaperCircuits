# L03 · Draw a resistor — teacher notes

Page: [out/L03-pencil-resistor.pdf](../../out/L03-pencil-resistor.pdf). Status: draft. Needs [P1](P1.md) in the binder.

## The idea

Pencil "lead" is graphite, and graphite conducts, but not well. A pencil line is a resistor you can draw. The longer the line, the more resistance; the narrower it is, the more resistance.

On the page:
- **Track A** is a 15 mm wide pencil track with the V+ lane running alongside it.
- **The wiper** is a cut-out card with copper underneath. Pressed across the lane and the track, it lets current into the track at that point.
- **The current's path:** from the wiper, current flows along the pencil to R1's lead, which lies across the track's end, then through R1 and LED1 to GND.
- **Dimming:** sliding the wiper away from R1 puts more pencil in the loop, so LED1 dims.
- **R1 (220 Ω)** protects LED1 if the wiper lands right on R1's lead and skips the pencil.
- **Track B** is 4 mm wide and only for measuring, so learners can compare width as well as length.

This sets up L04: real resistors do the same job in a small, fixed package, and the colour code says how much.

## What to expect

Pencil resistance varies a lot with the pencil, the paper, the pressure and the number of passes. Don't promise exact numbers; the ratios are what's reliable.

| Reading | Rough expectation |
|---|---|
| Track A, 10 mm | A few kΩ to a few tens of kΩ for heavy 6B |
| Track A, 20 / 40 / 80 mm | About 2×, 4× and 8× the 10 mm reading |
| Track B at the same length | About 4× track A (15 mm ÷ 4 mm ≈ 3.75), if both are shaded equally |

**LED brightness:** LED1's current is about (4.5 − 1.8) ÷ (220 Ω + track resistance).

| Wiper position | Track resistance | LED1 current | What learners see |
|---|---|---|---|
| On R1's lead | almost none | about 12 mA | full brightness |
| Near R1 | 5 kΩ | about 0.5 mA | a clear glow |
| Far from R1 | 50 kΩ | about 0.05 mA | barely visible, and only in a dim room |

This is why the parts list asks for a bright LED with a clear lens. The visible dimming happens over the first few centimetres; past that the LED is effectively off. That is the real behaviour of pencil, and worth pointing out.

## Common faults

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| LED1 never lights | Wiper upside down (copper must face the page); R1's lead not pressed onto the pencil; pencil too light (more passes); BT1 off |
| LED1 glows with no wiper | A pencil smudge across the gap between the V+ lane and track A. Rub it out. |
| LED1 is bright everywhere | The wiper is touching R1's lead, or the track's end is smudged onto R1's lane |
| Readings jump around | A light probe touch on pencil. Press the probe tips firmly and keep them still. |

## Extensions

- Shade a short track with an HB pencil on scrap card and measure it. HB has more clay and less graphite, so it reads far higher, often too high for the meter's 200 kΩ range.
- Draw a zig-zag track the same length as track A but folded into a smaller space. Does it measure the same?
