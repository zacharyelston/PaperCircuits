# L06 · Diodes are one-way valves — teacher notes

Page: [out/L06-diodes.pdf](../../out/L06-diodes.pdf). Status: draft. Needs [P1](P1.md) in the binder.

## The idea

A diode lets current through in one direction only, like a one-way valve. An LED is a diode that happens to glow, so learners have met this already in L01. Here they meet a plain diode and two new facts:

- **It costs about 0.7 V.** Current through a diode "uses up" about 0.6–0.7 V, which is why LED1 is a little dimmer than an LED with just a resistor.
- **The voltages round a loop add up to the supply.** Across R1, across D1 and across LED1 should total P1's voltage. This is Kirchhoff's voltage law, met with a meter rather than a name.

D2 is printed reversed on purpose. It blocks, so LED2 stays dark, and the whole supply appears across D2.

## Expected readings

With P1 at 4.5 V, black probe on TP6, corner held down:

| Point | Branch 1 (D1 forward) | Branch 2 (D2 reversed) |
|---|---|---|
| Before the diode (TP2 / TP4) | about 2.5 V | about 4.5 V: no current, so R2 drops nothing |
| After the diode (TP3 / TP5) | about 1.85 V, LED1's forward voltage | about 0 V |
| Across the diode | about 0.65 V | about 4.5 V |

**Branch 1 adds up:** about 2.0 V across R1, plus 0.65 V across D1, plus 1.85 V across LED1, makes 4.5 V. Its current is about (4.5 − 0.65 − 1.85) ÷ 220 ≈ 9 mA.

TP5 can read a fraction of a volt rather than exactly 0, from tiny leakage currents and the meter's own load. That's normal.

## Common faults

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Both LEDs light | D2 was placed band-down, the "right" way, instead of as printed |
| LED1 dark | D1 or LED1 backwards, or a lead not making contact |
| Can't find the band | A 1N4148 is small glass with a thin black band. Use a magnifier, or check it with the meter's diode range. |
